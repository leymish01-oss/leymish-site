Questions

What exactly changed on 1 October 2026?

Businesses can no longer add a surcharge to card payments on the eftpos, Mastercard and Visa networks. See the Reserve Bank's announcement and the Small Business Ombudsman's guide.

What about American Express, UnionPay and PayPal?

They aren't covered by the Reserve Bank's rules, but they agreed to stop card surcharges from the same date, 1 October 2026 (WA Consumer Protection, CommBank). If your terminal or website still adds one for Amex, ask your payment provider to switch it off.

Can I call it an "admin fee" or "processing fee" instead?

Not if only card payers pay it. A fee charged only on card payments is still a card surcharge under another name, and law firms warn that relabelling it can also be misleading under Australian Consumer Law. The check flags these fees when card payments are mentioned nearby.

Can I still charge more on public holidays?

Yes. A public holiday or weekend surcharge isn't a card surcharge. The Ombudsman says it must be clearly shown before the customer buys. The check lists those separately so you can see they're shown clearly.

Can I raise my prices instead?

Yes, as long as the new price is clear before purchase and you don't mislead customers about the reason for the increase.

Is this legal advice?

No. It's a search of your public pages and a writing service. For advice about your obligations, talk to the Ombudsman's office or a lawyer.

What can't the free check see?

Pages behind a login, Facebook and Instagram, your Google Business Profile, payment terminal settings, printed menus, booking emails, invoice templates, and PDFs that are scanned images. The clean-up covers those you send us.

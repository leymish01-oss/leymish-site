    The rules in short

    • Public holiday surcharges are still allowed. The ban from 1 October 2026 is on surcharges for paying with an eftpos, Mastercard or Visa card (American Express agreed to stop too). Weekend and public holiday surcharges aren't part of it.
    • Show it before people order. For restaurants and cafés, the ACCC says the menu must say "A surcharge of [percentage] applies on [day or days]", at least as prominently as the most prominent price on the menu. If your menu has no prices, show it in another prominent way.
    • Not a café or restaurant? That menu shortcut is described for restaurants and cafés. Other businesses should show the total public holiday price wherever it can be worked out (a public holiday price list works), as well as the notice.
    • Never call it a card fee or card surcharge, and don't charge it only to people who pay by card. A public holiday surcharge applies to everyone, however they pay.
    • Be honest about why. Don't give customers a reason for the surcharge that isn't true.
    • Check your part-day holidays. In South Australia and the Northern Territory, Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve are public holidays from 7pm to midnight; in Queensland, Christmas Eve from 6pm. A surcharge on those days should start at that time, and the wording above says so.

    Questions

    Can I still charge a surcharge on Christmas Day and Boxing Day?

    Yes. A public holiday surcharge isn't a card surcharge, so the 1 October 2026 ban doesn't stop it. It must be shown clearly before customers order, and on a café or restaurant menu the ACCC wording must be at least as prominent as the most prominent price.

    Which days are public holidays over Christmas 2026?

    Christmas Day is Friday 25 December and Boxing Day is Saturday 26 December. Because Boxing Day falls on a Saturday, every state and territory except Tasmania also has a public holiday on Monday 28 December; in Tasmania, Monday 28 December is the Boxing Day holiday instead of the Saturday. New Year's Day is Friday 1 January 2027.

    Are Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve public holidays?

    Only for part of the day, and only in some places: South Australia and the Northern Territory from 7pm to midnight on both days, and Queensland from 6pm to midnight on Christmas Eve. They aren't public holidays in the other states and territories.

    How big does the notice have to be?

    For restaurants and cafés, the ACCC says the words must be at least as prominent as the most prominent price on the menu. If your prices are in 14-point bold, the surcharge line should be at least 14-point bold too.

    Can I call it a card fee instead?

    No. A fee charged only to people who pay by card is a card surcharge, which is banned on eftpos, Mastercard and Visa from 1 October 2026. A public holiday surcharge applies to everyone, however they pay.

    Is this legal advice?

    No. It's a wording tool built from public guidance. For advice about your business, talk to the Small Business Ombudsman's office, your state's fair trading office or a lawyer.

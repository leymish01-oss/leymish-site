WooCommerce robots.txt for AI crawlers: what to allow

Short answer: Allow the crawlers that fetch pages for AI shopping answers; blocking training-only bots is your choice and doesn't stop you being recommended. Edit robots.txt from WordPress, make sure WordPress isn't set to discourage search engines, and test the result.

Every AI company now runs crawlers, and many store owners have started blocking them, sometimes on purpose, sometimes because a security plugin or a copied robots.txt did it for them. The catch is that not all AI crawlers do the same job. Some collect pages to train models. Others fetch pages so an assistant can answer a shopper's question with your product in it. Block the second kind and your products quietly drop out of AI shopping answers.

This guide explains which is which, gives you a robots.txt to copy, and shows where to edit it in WordPress.

Step 1: open your current robots.txt

Go to yourstore.com/robots.txt in your browser. WordPress creates this file automatically, and WooCommerce adds a few lines of its own. A fresh store's file looks like this:

Those default lines are fine: they keep crawlers out of the admin area, the add-to-cart links (which would otherwise fill your cart logs) and private upload folders. Nothing there blocks your products. What you're looking for is anything extra, especially a Disallow: / under an AI crawler's name or under User-agent: *.

Step 2: decide which AI crawlers to allow

Here are the crawlers that matter for a store, grouped by what they do.

Fetch pages for AI search and shopping answers. Allow these if you want to be recommended:

Crawler Company What it does
OAI-SearchBot OpenAI Reads pages for ChatGPT search and shopping results
ChatGPT-User OpenAI Fetches a page when a ChatGPT user asks about it
Claude-SearchBot Anthropic Reads pages for Claude's search answers
Claude-User Anthropic Fetches a page when a Claude user asks about it
PerplexityBot Perplexity Builds Perplexity's search index
Perplexity-User Perplexity Fetches a page when a Perplexity user asks about it
Googlebot Google Google Search, including AI Overviews and AI Mode
Bingbot Microsoft Bing, which Copilot's answers draw on

Collect pages for model training. Blocking these is your choice and doesn't stop the search crawlers above from recommending you:

Crawler Company What it does
GPTBot OpenAI Training data for OpenAI's models
ClaudeBot Anthropic Training data for Anthropic's models
Google-Extended Google Controls whether Google may use your pages for Gemini; it doesn't affect Google Search
Applebot-Extended Apple Controls whether Apple may use your pages for training
CCBot Common Crawl An open web archive that many AI models are trained on

One honest caveat: the user-triggered fetchers (ChatGPT-User, Claude-User, Perplexity-User) act for a person in real time, and their companies say they may not follow robots.txt for that. A firewall block still stops them. So if you want them in, robots.txt and your firewall both need to let them through.

A robots.txt you can copy

This keeps WooCommerce's sensible defaults, welcomes the search and shopping crawlers, and opts out of training. If you'd rather allow training too, delete the second block.

User-agent: *
Disallow: /wp-admin/
Allow: /wp-admin/admin-ajax.php
Disallow: /*?add-to-cart=
Disallow: /*?*add-to-cart=
Disallow: /wp-content/uploads/wc-logs/
Disallow: /wp-content/uploads/woocommerce_uploads/

User-agent: GPTBot
User-agent: ClaudeBot
User-agent: Google-Extended
User-agent: Applebot-Extended
User-agent: CCBot
Disallow: /

Sitemap: https://yourstore.com/wp-sitemap.xml

You don't need a separate block for OAI-SearchBot and the others: anything not named gets the User-agent: * rules, which allow your products. Change the sitemap line to your real sitemap address. If you use an SEO plugin, it's usually /sitemap_index.xml.

Step 3: edit robots.txt in WordPress

WordPress doesn't have a robots.txt editor of its own. You have three options:

  • Your SEO plugin. Yoast SEO has it under Yoast SEO → Tools → File editor. Rank Math has it under Rank Math → General Settings → Edit robots.txt. Most other SEO plugins have something similar.
  • A real file. Upload a file called robots.txt to your site's root folder (the one with wp-config.php) with your host's file manager or SFTP. A real file replaces the automatic one completely, so include WooCommerce's default lines.
  • Ask your host. Some managed hosts control robots.txt for you, especially on staging sites.

Step 4: check Search engine visibility is off

There's one more switch that affects every crawler at once. Go to Settings → Reading and make sure Discourage search engines from indexing this site is not ticked. When it's ticked, WordPress asks all search engines to skip your pages, and AI crawlers follow that too.

Step 5: test the result

Reload yourstore.com/robots.txt to see the new rules. If they haven't changed, a caching plugin or CDN may be serving the old file: clear the cache and try again.

Then check the firewall side. In your security plugin, look for settings like "block bad bots" or "block AI crawlers". If you use Cloudflare, check its AI crawler and bot settings. If these block the search crawlers, your robots.txt can be perfect and the crawlers still get an error page.

Common mistakes

  • Copying a "block all AI" list. Many lists online mix training bots and search bots. Blocking OAI-SearchBot or PerplexityBot costs you visibility.
  • Disallow: / left over from building the site. Developers often block everything before launch and forget to remove it.
  • Blocking /wp-content/uploads/ completely. That folder holds your product images. AI answers and Google Shopping both show images.

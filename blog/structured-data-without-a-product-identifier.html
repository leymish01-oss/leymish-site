Your product schema is probably fine. Your product identifier probably isn't.

There is a comfortable way to be invisible to AI shopping agents: do the structured data properly and stop there.

In late September we checked 100 WooCommerce stores from the WooCommerce showcase, using only public signals — the same things any crawler sees. We could reach a product page on 76 of them. 55 of those 76 had Product structured data. That is 72%, and it is a genuinely good number; it is far better than the web average.

Then we looked at what was inside the structured data.

  • 4 of the 76 carried a GTIN or an MPN.
  • 2 carried a GTIN specifically.
  • 12 named a brand.
  • 49 carried a SKU.

So of the 55 stores that had done the structured-data work, 51 had no product identifier in it at all. They had built the box and left it empty.

Why a SKU is not an identifier

A SKU is yours. You invented it, it means something inside your warehouse, and nobody outside your business can map BLU-TSHIRT-M-02 onto anything. That is exactly what makes it useless to an agent that is trying to work out whether your product is the same thing another shop is selling for less.

49 of 76 stores had a SKU and only 4 had a GTIN or MPN. That gap is the whole problem in one line: stores are publishing an internal code and assuming it does the job of an external one.

The two identifiers that travel:

  • GTIN — the barcode number. EAN, UPC, ISBN and JAN are all GTINs. It comes from the manufacturer, or from GS1 if you are the manufacturer. You cannot make one up.
  • MPN — the manufacturer's part number, with a brand alongside it. Brand plus MPN is the fallback when there is no barcode, and for a lot of WooCommerce stores it is the realistic answer.

Google's Merchant Center has wanted these for years. The newer AI shopping surfaces want them for the same reason: without one, your product is a string of words that may or may not be the thing the shopper asked for, and an agent that has to guess will usually pick the listing it does not have to guess about.

What this looks like on a WooCommerce store

WooCommerce 9.2 added GTIN, UPC, EAN and ISBN as a built-in field on the product's Inventory tab, so on a current install you do not need a plugin for the identifier itself. The two things worth checking:

  1. Is the field filled in? A field that exists and is empty publishes nothing.
  2. Does your theme put it in the JSON-LD? This is where most of the 51 stores lose it. WooCommerce knows the value; whether it reaches the Product schema on the page depends on what is generating that schema — the theme, an SEO plugin, or WooCommerce itself. Having all three is a common reason a product ends up with two Product blocks that disagree.

The way to check is to open a product page, view source, find the application/ld+json block, and look for gtin, mpn and brand. If the block is there and those keys are not, you are one of the 51.

Brand is the cheap half of this

12 of 76 named a brand. For a single-brand store that is a one-line fix with an enormous ratio of effort to effect, because brand plus mpn is a valid identifier pair on its own. If you sell your own products, you are the brand, and leaving it out is pure loss.

What not to do

Do not generate GTINs. We say this from having had the conversation on a real store: when we fixed a skincare store in September, 24 of 24 products had no identifier, and inventing barcodes would have been the fast way to make the audit score go up. A fabricated GTIN is worse than none — it either collides with a real product or fails validation, and in Merchant Center it gets the product disapproved. We gave that store SKUs, MPNs and a brand instead, and left the GTIN field empty where no barcode existed.

An empty GTIN field is an honest statement that the product has no barcode. That is allowed. A wrong one is not.

The order to do it in

  1. Add brand to every product. One field, whole catalogue, works immediately.
  2. Add MPNs for anything with a manufacturer part number.
  3. Add GTINs only where you have the real barcode, from the box or from the manufacturer.
  4. View source on three product pages and confirm the values actually reach the JSON-LD.

Step 4 is the one people skip, and it is the one that decides whether any of the first three were worth doing.

The method, in case you want to repeat it

We took the stores from the public WooCommerce showcase, which makes this a sample of well-maintained, established stores rather than a random slice of the web — if anything, these numbers flatter the ecosystem. We made at most 5 requests per store, obeyed robots.txt, and read only what any crawler reads. Full method and the opt-out: /woocommerce/census.html.
